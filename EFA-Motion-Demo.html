Assumptions, interpretation, and references

The analogy. Movement on the screen stands for covariation across people: items that move together are items whose scores rise and fall together in a sample. EFA works with the correlation matrix of the items, not with change over time.

Common and unique variance. A factor model splits each item’s variance into a part shared with other items (communality) and a unique part. The noise slider adds unique variation; the speed slider strengthens the common influence.

Interpretation. A factor summarizes shared variance. Reading it as a common cause is a theoretical claim; the same correlations can sometimes be produced by other structures, such as items that influence each other directly.

The animation and loadings are illustrative. The references below credit the methods; no figures or text from them are reproduced.
