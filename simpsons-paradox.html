The pooled slope is positive, but within every age group it is negative. Which slope answers the question “Among people of the same age, is more exercise associated with lower cholesterol?”

Assumptions, interpretation, and references

Simulation. Three age groups of 70 people. Older groups exercise more on average and have higher cholesterol on average. Within each group, cholesterol changes by the chosen slope for each extra hour of weekly exercise, plus random noise. The numbers are illustrative, not estimates from real data.

Pooled and within-group slopes. The pooled slope is an ordinary regression on all 210 people. The within-group slope is the regression slope after removing each group’s means, which equals the exercise coefficient in a regression that also includes age group.

Which one to report. The data alone cannot say. If age influences both exercise and cholesterol (a confounder), the within-age slope is the relevant one. If the grouping variable were instead a consequence of exercise (a mediator or collider), adjusting for it could mislead. The choice depends on the causal structure assumed.
