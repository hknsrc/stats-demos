The slope of X is significant at W = +1 SD but not at W = −1 SD. Does this, on its own, show a significant X × W interaction?

Assumptions, interpretation, and references

Simple slope. The effect of X at a given W is θ(W) = b₁ + b₃W, with standard error √(Var(b₁) + 2W·Cov(b₁, b₃) + W²·Var(b₃)). The Johnson–Neyman boundaries are the values of W where θ(W) / SE(W) equals the critical t value (df = N − 4).

Simplifications in this demo. X and W are standardized, uncorrelated, and normally distributed, and the residual SD is 1. Then Var(b₁) ≈ Var(b₃) ≈ 1/N and Cov(b₁, b₃) ≈ 0. In real data, use the estimated covariance matrix of the coefficients; statistical software reports the boundaries directly.

Reading the plot. The interaction test is the test of b₃: it asks whether the slope changes with W. The region of significance asks a different question: at which W the slope differs from zero. Pick-a-point values (±1 SD) are arbitrary; the Johnson–Neyman plot shows all values at once.
