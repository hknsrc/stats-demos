Check your understanding

One study reports a 95% confidence interval of [3.1, 7.4] for a mean. Which statement is correct?

Assumptions, interpretation, and references

The interval. Each interval is x̄ ± tn−1 · s / √n, where s is the sample standard deviation. Samples are drawn from a normal population with μ = 100 and σ = 15, so the t interval has exactly the stated coverage.

Interpretation. The confidence level describes the procedure: in repeated sampling, that share of intervals would contain μ. Once one interval is computed, it either contains μ or it does not; the level is not a probability statement about that particular interval in the frequentist framework. Bayesian credible intervals answer a different question and require a prior.

When coverage fails. With skewed populations, small samples, or dependent observations, actual coverage can differ from the nominal level.
