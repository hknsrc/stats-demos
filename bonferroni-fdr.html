FWER · FAMILY-WISE ERROR RATE

“Could any rejection be false?”

Bonferroni limits the probability of at least one false positive in the test family. With α = 0.05, that probability is at most 5%; it need not equal 5%.

FDR · FALSE DISCOVERY RATE

“What share of discoveries is false?”

FDR is the expected proportion of false positives among rejected hypotheses. Under suitable assumptions, BH controls this expectation at q. The realized proportion in a single study can be higher.

Bonferroni: padj = min(1, m × p). BH: for sorted p-values, take the cumulative minimum from the largest rank backward: padj,(i) = min(1, minj ≥ i[m × p(j) / j]). Compare adjusted p-values with 0.05.

No qualifying BH rank? If no p(i) satisfies p(i) ≤ iq/m, reject none. The present example has k = 5.

Assumptions, interpretation, and references

Assumptions. With valid individual p-values, Bonferroni provides strong FWER control regardless of dependence among tests. Standard BH controls FDR under independence and specific positive-dependence conditions; arbitrary correlation does not automatically satisfy these conditions. Under more general dependence, procedures such as Benjamini–Yekutieli (BY) may be considered.

Power. Stricter error control can increase missed effects. FDR control does not repair an underpowered design: sample size, effect size, and measurement quality still matter. When FWER control is the goal, Holm is an alternative that is at least as powerful as classical Bonferroni.

The test family. A family groups tests considered together for an inferential purpose. Membership should follow the research claims and analysis plan, rather than merely the placement of tests in a table. Test count alone does not determine the appropriate error target.
