Assumptions, interpretation, and references

The model. Classical test theory writes each observed score as a true score plus random error, X = T + E, with errors uncorrelated with true scores and with each other. Reliability is the share of observed-score variance that is true-score variance: rxx = Var(T) / Var(X). Under these assumptions the observed correlation equals ρT · √(rxx · ryy).

Limits of the correction. Correlated or systematic errors (for example, shared method variance) can inflate rather than shrink an observed correlation, and the correction does not address them. Corrected values depend on which reliability coefficient is used (for example, internal consistency versus test–retest) and inherit its sampling error. Report the observed and corrected values side by side.

Why it matters. Unreliable measures lower statistical power and bias standardized effects toward zero. Latent-variable models (CFA/SEM) address the same problem by separating common variance from item-specific error.
