Assumptions, interpretation, and references

The model. The three-parameter logistic model: P(θ) = c + (1 − c) / (1 + e−a(θ − b)). With c = 0 it reduces to the two-parameter model, and with a common a to the Rasch / one-parameter model. Some texts include a scaling constant D = 1.7 in the exponent; this demo does not.

Information. Item information is I(θ) = a2 · [(P − c)2 / (1 − c)2] · [(1 − P) / P]. Without guessing it peaks at θ = b with value a2/4; guessing lowers the peak and moves it slightly above b. Test information is the sum over items, and SE(θ) ≈ 1/√I(θ).

Assumptions. The curves assume one latent trait (unidimensionality), local independence of items given θ, and a correctly specified curve shape. Parameters are estimated from data and have their own uncertainty.

Item parameters are illustrative. The references below credit the methods; no figures or text from them are reproduced.
