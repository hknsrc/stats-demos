People with the highest blood pressure at a screening are measured again a week later. They received no treatment. On average, what do you expect at the second measurement?

Assumptions, interpretation, and references

Simulation. Each person has a stable true score. Each measurement adds independent error, so the correlation between the two measurements equals r. Scores have mean 100 and SD 15 at both occasions. Without a treatment, nothing changes on average.

Why it happens. People selected for very low first scores tend to have had unlucky errors that time. Their errors at the second measurement are, on average, zero, so their scores move back toward the mean. With equal variances, the expected change is (1 − r) times the distance of the group’s first mean from the overall mean.

Dealing with it. Use a randomized control group selected the same way, take several baseline measurements, or analyze change with methods that model the selection. A pre–post change in an extreme group alone cannot separate treatment effects from regression to the mean.
