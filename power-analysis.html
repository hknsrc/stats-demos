Assumptions, interpretation, and references

The setting. Two independent groups of equal size n, normally distributed outcomes with equal variances, and a two-sided test. Under H₀ the standardized test statistic is centred at 0; under H₁ it is centred at δ = d · √(n/2). Power is P(|Z| > z1−α/2) under H₁.

Choosing d. Power depends on the true effect, which is unknown. Effect sizes from small published studies tend to be overestimated, so planning with them gives optimistic power. A smallest effect size of interest is often a better basis.

Low power and published results. When power is low, significant results are more likely to overestimate the effect, and a larger share of them can be false positives.
