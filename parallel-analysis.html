Assumptions, interpretation, and references

Simulation. Each item loads on one factor with the chosen loading; factors are uncorrelated and items are split into equal blocks. Unique variances make each item’s variance 1. With 0 factors, all items are independent.

Eigenvalues. The demo uses eigenvalues of the full correlation matrix (principal components), as in Horn’s original method. Variants use the reduced correlation matrix (communalities on the diagonal) or the mean instead of the 95th percentile; they can give different answers.

Parallel analysis. For each position, the observed eigenvalue is compared with the 95th percentile of eigenvalues from 60 random normal data sets with the same N and number of items. Components are kept until the first one that falls below.

Other evidence. Velicer’s MAP test, model fit of competing solutions, and whether each factor makes substantive sense are commonly used alongside parallel analysis.

Data are simulated for teaching. The references below credit the methods; no figures or text from them are reproduced.
