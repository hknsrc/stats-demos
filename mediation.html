Assumptions, interpretation, and references

Simulation. X is standard normal. M = aX + error and Y = c′X + bM + error, with standard normal errors. Estimates come from ordinary least squares in one simulated sample of size N: a from regressing M on X, b and c′ from regressing Y on X and M, and c from regressing Y on X alone. In linear models estimated this way, c = c′ + ab holds exactly in the sample.

Bootstrap. The sample is resampled with replacement 1,000 times; a × b is re-estimated each time. The 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles form the 95% percentile interval. The bootstrap is preferred over the Sobel test because the sampling distribution of a × b is usually skewed.

Older rules. The causal-steps approach required a significant total effect before testing mediation. Current recommendations test the indirect effect directly, because the total effect can be small when direct and indirect effects have opposite signs, or when the study has low power for c.
