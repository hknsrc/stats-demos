Assumptions, interpretation, and references

The model. Items are standardized. In the one-factor case, item j has loading λj and unique variance 1 − λj2; errors are uncorrelated. In the two-factor case, the first half of the items load on factor 1 and the rest on factor 2, and the factors correlate φ.

Formulas. α = k / (k − 1) · (1 − Σσj2 / σX2), where σX2 is the variance of the total score. ω = (variance of the total score explained by the factors) / σX2; with one factor, ω = (Σλ)2 / [(Σλ)2 + Σ(1 − λ2)]. In the two-factor case the demo shows ω total, which counts both factors as true-score variance.

When α and ω differ. With one factor and uncorrelated errors, α ≤ ω, with equality when all loadings are equal. Correlated errors (for example, similarly worded items) can push α above the reliability it is meant to estimate. Neither coefficient tests whether a scale is unidimensional; that requires a factor analysis.
