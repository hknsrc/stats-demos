Assumptions, interpretation, and references

The continuum. Self-Determination Theory distinguishes amotivation, four types of extrinsic motivation (external, introjected, identified, integrated), and intrinsic motivation. External and introjected regulation are called controlled; identified, integrated, and intrinsic are called autonomous. People usually hold several types at once.

Internalization. Support for autonomy, competence, and relatedness helps people take in the value of a behavior, moving their reasons toward the autonomous end. This is a tendency, not a fixed sequence of stages.

Weight-related reasons. Reasons tied to appearance or self-worth usually count as introjected. They are common and understandable, and they tend to be less durable than reasons based on personal value or enjoyment.
