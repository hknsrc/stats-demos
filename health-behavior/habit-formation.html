How long does it take to form a habit?

A habit is a behavior that starts almost automatically when its cue appears. Automaticity grows with each repetition, quickly at first and then more slowly.

Assumptions, interpretation, and references

The evidence. In a 12-week study, participants chose a daily eating, drinking, or activity behavior in a consistent context and rated its automaticity each day. Automaticity rose along an asymptotic curve. The median time to reach 95% of each person’s plateau was 66 days, with a range of 18 to 254 days; activity behaviors tended to take longer than drinking. Missing a single opportunity did not materially affect the process.

The model. Each walk adds a fixed share of the remaining distance to the maximum; a missed day removes a small share. The share is larger for simple behaviors and smaller when the behavior is not tied to a stable cue. The parameters are illustrative.

Habit and goals. Habits form through repetition of a behavior in a stable context. Once formed, they can continue with less conscious effort. Intentions still matter for starting and for choosing which behaviors to repeat.
