Assumptions, interpretation, and references

The theory. In the Theory of Planned Behavior, attitude, subjective norm, and perceived behavioral control predict intention; intention and perceived control predict behavior. Across behaviors, the model explains a substantial share of variance in intention and a smaller share in behavior.

In this demo. Intention is a weighted average of the three beliefs with weights .45 (attitude), .10 (norm), and .35 (perceived control), reflecting the common finding that norms are weaker predictors of physical activity intentions. The 100 students have intentions spread around Jordan’s. Their chance of walking rises with intention and perceived control; an if–then plan raises it for those who intend.

Limits. The theory says little about why intentions fail, which led to models of action control and to planning interventions. Effects of if–then plans vary across studies and are smaller when people are less committed to the goal.
