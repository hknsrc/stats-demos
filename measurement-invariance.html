Assumptions, interpretation, and references

The model. For item j in group g, y = τjg + λjgη + ε. Group A is the reference group: its latent mean is fixed at 0 and both latent variances are 1 in this demo. The expected sum score in a group is Σ(τj + λjκ), where κ is that group’s latent mean.

Levels. Configural: same number of factors and same pattern of loadings. Metric (weak): equal loadings, which supports comparing factor variances, covariances, and regression slopes. Scalar (strong): equal loadings and intercepts, which supports comparing latent means. Strict invariance adds equal residual variances and is needed if observed-score variances are compared; it is not shown here.

Partial invariance. When one or two parameters differ, they can be freed while the rest stay equal. Latent means are then identified by the invariant items. Freeing parameters should have a substantive justification, not only a fit-based one.

Testing. Researchers usually compare nested models with χ² difference tests and changes in fit indices such as ΔCFI. Fit-change cutoffs are rules of thumb and depend on sample size and model size.
