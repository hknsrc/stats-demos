Check your understanding

A researcher tests after every 10 participants per group and stops at the first p < .05. There is no true effect. What is the chance of ending with a significant result?

Assumptions, interpretation, and references

Simulation. Each study draws observations for two groups from the same normal distribution (σ = 1), so every significant result is a false positive. At each look, a two-sided z-test compares the group means, with n participants per group. The false-positive rates are based on 2,000 simulated studies.

Why the rate rises. Each look is another chance to cross the threshold, and successive tests are correlated but not identical. With unlimited looks, a study would eventually reach p < .05 with probability approaching 1.

Alternatives. Fix the sample size in advance, or plan a sequential design with a stated number of looks and adjusted thresholds. Preregistration makes the stopping rule visible to readers.
