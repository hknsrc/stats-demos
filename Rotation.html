Assumptions, interpretation, and references

What rotation keeps. Rotation changes the position of the axes, not the items. Item communalities and the fit of the factor model are the same before and after rotation; only the way the common variance is divided between factors changes.

Orthogonal and oblique. Orthogonal rotations (for example, varimax) keep the factors uncorrelated. Oblique rotations (for example, oblimin, promax, geomin) allow a factor correlation, which equals the cosine of the angle between the axes. If the estimated correlation is near zero, the oblique and orthogonal solutions will look similar.

Pattern and structure. The table shows pattern coefficients, which are usually reported as loadings: each item is projected onto one axis parallel to the other axis. A perpendicular projection gives the structure coefficient (the item–factor correlation) instead. The two are equal only when the factors are uncorrelated; with correlated factors, structure coefficients stay larger on the secondary factor.
